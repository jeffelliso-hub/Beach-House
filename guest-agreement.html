Charging the Electric Cart
The cart is electric with lead-acid batteries. I will plug it in to charge when not in use — a full charge takes up to 8 hours. Keeping it charged is my responsibility; a $100 service fee applies if it becomes inoperable due to inadequate charging.
Caution: The garage door must remain open while charging — lead-acid batteries produce hydrogen gas, and charging in an enclosed space is a fire and health hazard. Do not leave charging unattended.
Caution: Do not operate the cart during a rainstorm or through standing water. Doing so is an operating error and may result in a $200 fee for water damage.
Road Rules (NC & Oak Island)
Golf cart driving laws are the same as motor vehicle laws in North Carolina. I will follow all laws of NC, Brunswick County, and the Town of Oak Island.
The cart may only be driven on roads posted at 35 mph or below. It is not permitted to cross any bridge leaving Oak Island, and is not permitted on sidewalks, the beach, in sand or surf, or through standing water.
No driving under the influence of alcohol or drugs, and no open containers of alcohol on the cart. No smoking on the cart.
At the beach front, the cart may only park in designated Town of Oak Island parking spaces. I am responsible for all moving and parking violations while the cart is in my possession.
The cart will not carry more passengers than the number of seat belts available.
